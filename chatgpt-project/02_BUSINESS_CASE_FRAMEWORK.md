# Business Case Framework

A complete business case should include:

1. Executive Summary
2. Problem Statement and Business Need
3. Strategic Alignment
4. Current State Assessment
5. Options Analysis
6. Recommended Solution
7. Financial Analysis
8. Risk Assessment
9. Implementation Roadmap
10. Governance and Decision Framework
11. Recommendation and Call to Action

## Drafting order

Draft in dependency order, not output order:

Problem -> Current state -> Options -> Recommendation -> Financials -> Risk -> Roadmap -> Governance -> Executive Summary -> Call to action.

## Required discipline

- Do Nothing must be a real option with cost of inaction.
- Financial claims require assumptions.
- Soft benefits stay out of the ROI headline.
- Classify each benefit as hard financial, cost avoidance, revenue protection, mission value, risk reduction, quality/rework, cycle time, customer/employee experience, compliance/control, or strategic option value.
- Separate ROI from value. ROI needs validated cost and financial benefit logic; other value classes may justify action without being called ROI.
- Name baseline availability, confidence, finance-validation status, and cost of doing nothing for each material claim.
- Every material benefit claim should carry a measurement contract: expected outcome, benefit type, metric, baseline, target, actual if available, measurement period, source, measure owner, review cadence, validation need, confidence, realization risk, finance-sensitive flag, and downstream route.
- Risks must be named, not categorized vaguely.
- The call to action must name the decision, decision-maker, deadline, and consequence of delay.
